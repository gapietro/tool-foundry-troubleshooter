# ServiceNow Brand Standards Reference

These are the approved ServiceNow brand standards. Apply them to **every piece of content** Claude
creates for or about ServiceNow—copy, documents, presentations, artifacts, dashboards, visualizations,
images, PNGs, emails, social posts, and any other branded output.

This skill is the **foundation layer**. Downstream skills (Corporate PPTX, Word Doc, HTML/Artifact, etc.)
inherit these standards as their default. Voice & tone rules are non-negotiable everywhere. Visual design
rules may be overridden by downstream skills only when explicitly documented—see Section 4 for the
full inheritance model.

## 1. Voice & Tone

### 1.1 Brand Positioning (Ground Every Piece of Writing Here)

- **Who we are:** The AI control tower for business reinvention
- **Why we exist:** To make the world work better for everyone
- **What we do:** Turn friction into flow across every corner of the enterprise
- **How we do it:** Put AI to work for people

### 1.2 Brand Personality

ServiceNow's personality is expressed through four traits. These inform the attitude behind everything we write:

- **Founded on empathy**—Technology exists to serve people, not replace them
- **Fueled by tech optimism**—We see possibility everywhere
- **Marked by bold ambition**—We aim to be the defining enterprise company of the 21st century
- **With a mandate to keep it real**—Simple language, no corporate fog, people in mind

### 1.3 The Three Voice Traits

Every piece of ServiceNow content must express **at least one** of these three voice traits. They are constant—they never change regardless of format or channel.

**Positively curious**
We believe imagination is the limit. Our joy and curiosity help us explain complex things simply, with a playful energy that's ours to own.
- Writing tip: Approach each topic with openness. Ask thought-provoking questions, highlight possibilities, inspire audiences to imagine what's next. Take pride in simplifying the complex.
- Example: "AI Agents are built into the ServiceNow platform we already use. They're literally in every corner of our business."

**People obsessed**
If it doesn't make life better for people, it doesn't belong here. We make technology and AI work alongside people, not in replacement of them.
- Writing tip: Put people at the center of the message. Speak directly to the audience's needs and aspirations. Show empathy in words and solutions.
- Example: "Let ServiceNow AI Agents sweat the small stuff so your people can shine."

**Refreshingly direct**
We avoid speaking "AI bingo" and adding to the industry swirl. We speak clearly and confidently, with language that connects to our audience.
- Writing tip: Communicate clearly, honestly, and without unnecessary jargon. Value the audience's time by keeping messages straightforward and easy to understand.
- Example: "AI helps Jim solve customer problems before they're problems." / "For reals?" / "For reals."

### 1.4 How Tone Flexes

Voice is constant. **Tone** reflects context—it changes depending on the purpose of the communication and the audience's emotional state. Think of tone as adjustable dials.

**ServiceNow's natural center of gravity across six tone dimensions:**

- Bold ←——●———→ Humble (leans bold)
- Playful ←———●——→ Serious (slightly playful of center)
- Human ←——●———→ Technical (leans human)
- Conversational ←———●——→ Corporate (slightly conversational of center)
- Contrarian ←———●——→ Conventional (near center, slightly contrarian)
- Forward-Looking ←———●——→ Reflective (slightly forward-looking of center)

**Adjusting tone by context:**
- Product marketing → pull more toward Technical
- Social media → pull further toward Playful
- Financial analyst day or press release → pull toward Serious and Corporate
- Brand campaigns and video → pull toward Lighthearted and Bold
- Customer stories → pull toward Human and Conversational

**The rule:** When serious, be buttoned up but never boring. When lighthearted, be silly and clever but never smart-ass.

### 1.5 General Writing Rules

**Focus on the benefit.** Customers care about how a product impacts them, not technical specs. Never stray far from the human benefit.
- ✅ "Your people who build now build faster than ever."
- ❌ Leading with feature lists or spec sheets

**Be clear above all else.** Our category is complex but our audience are people. Speak simply. Use everyday words. Use short sentences where possible.
- ✅ "Put AI to work across every corner of your business."

**Avoid jargon.** Limit reliance on acronyms. Speak with simple, familiar, conversational language.
- ✅ "Drive revenue with better employee and customer experiences."
- ❌ "With EXP and CX, TTR is down and ROI is up."

**Write with energy.** Be bold. Write in a manner that makes the audience feel inspired and energized.
- ✅ "AI is only as powerful as the platform it's built into."

**Be conversational.** Write like you speak. Use "we" and "us" when talking about ServiceNow. Use "you" when speaking to the audience.
- ✅ "Get more done faster. How fast? That fast."

**No spaces around dashes.** Em dashes (—) and en dashes (–) are set tight—no space before or after.
- ✅ "Accent only—highlights, never background"
- ❌ "Accent only — highlights, never background" (no spaces around the dash)

### 1.6 Advanced Writing Rules (For Longer-Form Content)

Apply these when creating narrative content—brand videos, social campaigns, case studies, thought leadership, or anywhere with space to develop ideas.

**Keep humans at the center.** AI exists to elevate people, not erase them. Always bring focus to how technology enables human capability.
- ✅ "Let people spend less time on the stuff they don't love, and more time on the stuff they do."

**Use humor as a tool.** Comedy makes enterprise software feel human. It isn't cute for its own sake—use it to make the complex feel approachable.

**Write with emotional intelligence.** Have empathy for teams, appreciation for partners, pride in employees, and enthusiasm for collaboration.

**Make the future feel real.** Stay optimistic but ground it in tangible workflows and tasks.
- ✅ "On the ServiceNow AI platform, there's a better way to CRM. From logistics to customer history, issues don't just get tracked, they get resolved."

**Connect back to the brand.** "Put AI to Work For People" underpins all communications—either explicitly or woven implicitly throughout.

### 1.7 Writing Craft Principles

**Declarative confidence.** Lead with bold, definitive statements. Eliminate hedging words: "maybe," "could," "might," "we believe," "we think." Own the position.
- ✅ "You don't need AI agents."
- ✅ "Bad CRM was then. This is ServiceNow."
- ❌ "We think ServiceNow offers a better approach."
- Litmus test: If you can add "maybe" or "probably" to the sentence, rewrite it stronger.

**Human-centric language.** Write like one human talking to another. Reference real human details. Use conversational asides. Choose simple words over corporate terminology.
- ✅ "Oh, and you still find time to fix all your coworkers' problems, even if it's just power cycling a laptop for the third time."
- ❌ "Our solution provides enhanced operational efficiency."
- Litmus test: Would a real person say this in conversation, or does it sound like a press release?

**Strategic provocation.** Challenge industry assumptions. Identify the prevailing belief, then flip it. Create productive tension.
- ✅ "You don't need AI agents." (then explaining why agents need people)
- ✅ "Can your AI agents do more than just chat?"
- ❌ "AI agents are an important tool for businesses."
- Litmus test: Does this make someone pause and reconsider what they thought they knew?

**Elevate, don't transcribe.** Transform strategy inputs into on-brand copy. The brief gives you ingredients—you create the dish.
- ❌ Brief language: "AI agents that work autonomously across departments"
- ✅ Ad copy: "They don't live. They don't dream. But people do."
- ❌ Brief language: "ServiceNow makes people more productive by automating repetitive tasks"
- ✅ Ad copy: "Rediscover the job you signed up for."

**Use specifics, not generics.** Name the actual work. Paint the picture.
- ✅ "password resets, invoice approvals, ticket routing"
- ✅ "big ideas scribbled on colorful sticky notes"
- ❌ "handle various tasks" / "automate processes" / "improve efficiency"

**Compress ruthlessly.** Every word must earn its place. If you can cut it without losing meaning, cut it.
- ❌ "ServiceNow provides a comprehensive platform that enables organizations to leverage AI agents for automating workflows across multiple departments"
- ✅ "Built in, not bolted on."

### 1.8 Sentence Architecture & Rhythm Patterns

**Vary length dramatically.** Mix short, punchy sentences with longer flowing ones. Create rhythm through contrast.

**Pattern: Short. Short. Medium.**
"We know. Sounds weird. Especially from the leader in AI agents."

**Pattern: Fragment. Fragment. Complete sentence.**
"Not chatbots. Not task assistants. Complete workflows."

**Pattern: Repetition for rhythm.**
"They don't live. They don't dream. And they certainly don't have weird dogs, teenagers, favorite shows..."

**Pattern: Parallel structure.**
"From pilot to production. From roadmap to reality."

**Pattern: Question? Answer. Proof.**
"How fast? That fast."

**The Stops/Starts pattern (highly effective for showing transformation):**
"Your service desk stops firefighting and starts fixing systems."
"Your finance team stops closing books and starts spotting opportunities."

**Pattern: Philosophy → Specific → Resolution.**
1. Open with provocative truth: "Autonomy doesn't belong to AI. It belongs to your people."
2. Get specific: "When AI agents autonomously handle the routine—password resets, invoice approvals, ticket routing—something shifts."
3. Close with what it means for people: "Your people operate at higher altitude, shaping outcomes, applying judgment, creating impact."

### 1.9 Proven Headline Patterns

- **The Contrarian Flip:** State something unexpected, then reveal the deeper truth. "You don't need AI agents."
- **The Temporal Marker:** Draw a line in time, position as the modern solution. "Bad CRM was then. This is ServiceNow." CRITICAL: Only use for major category-defining moments—this is NOT a fill-in-the-blank template.
- **The Provocative Question:** Expose competitor weakness with implied "no" answer. "Can your AI agents talk to each other?"
- **The Proof-Led Statement:** Lead with undeniable proof. "85% of the Fortune 500 share one thing."
- **The Direct Benefit:** Simple, clear transformation. "Rediscover the job you signed up for."
- **The Poetic Truth:** Emotional resonance through unexpected phrasing. "A digital labor force doesn't dream."

### 1.10 Dos and Don'ts

**DO:**
- ✅ Keep it short and sweet with a touch of wit
- ✅ Write in active voice, celebrating the benefit
- ✅ Use hopeful, positive language to inspire
- ✅ Use emotive, action-driven verbs
- ✅ Use "people" (never "humans")
- ✅ Use first-person plural ("we"/"us") for ServiceNow, second-person ("you") for audience
- ✅ Lead with proof, then make claims

**DON'T:**
- ❌ Rely on puns or forced wordplay ("Go from admin to radmin")
- ❌ Use passive voice to bury the benefit
- ❌ Overpromise or make unrealistic claims ("Conquer any challenge. Anywhere, anytime.")
- ❌ Force emotion with exclamation points ("Easily create cutting-edge apps!")
- ❌ Use "humans"—always say "people"
- ❌ Name competitors ("Unlike Salesforce..." / "Better than competitors...")
- ❌ Make AI or technology the hero—people are always the heroes, technology is the enabler
- ❌ Use internal strategy language in final copy ("Our differentiator is..." / "The narrative is...")
- ❌ Use technical jargon in external content: L1/L2/L3, TTR, SLA, MTTR, internal product codes
- ❌ Use "digital workers" or "digital workforce" (exception: "digital labor force" in poetic context)
- ❌ String together acronyms without explanation

### 1.11 Competitive Positioning Rules

Always allude to category problems. Never name competitors.
- ✅ "If it looks like a chatbot and talks like a chatbot, it's a chatbot."
- ✅ "Most AI is stuck in silos. Trapped in apps."
- ❌ "Unlike Salesforce..." / "Better than [competitor]..." / "While other platforms..."

### 1.12 ServiceNow Signature Language

Use these phrases authentically when relevant:
- "Put AI to work for people"
- "Built in, not bolted on"
- "Every corner of your business" / "Every corner of the enterprise"
- "The world works with ServiceNow"
- "Make the world work better for everyone"
- "Rediscover the job you signed up for"

### 1.13 Temperature Check

Before finalizing any content, gut-check the temperature:

- **TOO COLD** (feature-focused, corporate): "ServiceNow provides AI agents for cross-departmental workflow automation."
- **JUST RIGHT** (bold but grounded, poetic but clear): "They don't live. They don't dream. But people do."
- **TOO HOT** (overselling, forced enthusiasm): "Revolutionary AI agents will totally transform and disrupt everything!!!"

The sweet spot: Bold and confident, but grounded. Poetic but clear. Provocative but not trying too hard.

### 1.14 Final Checklist (Apply to Every Piece of Content)

Before publishing, verify:
- [ ] Does this sound like ServiceNow—not generic, not like another brand?
- [ ] Is at least one voice trait present (positively curious, people obsessed, refreshingly direct)?
- [ ] Are people the heroes and technology the enabler?
- [ ] Would someone outside our industry understand this?
- [ ] Does the tone match the situation and audience?
- [ ] Is the "for people" philosophy present—explicitly or implicitly?
- [ ] Have I cut every unnecessary word?
- [ ] Am I using specific examples, not generic language?
- [ ] Does this avoid ALL critical violations (jargon, competitor names, passive voice, hedging)?

## 2. Color

### 2.1 Primary Palette

Primary colors are Infinite Blue and Wasabi Green, along with essential neutrals. Infinite Blue should be used most frequently. Wasabi Green is an accent color—a little goes a long way, so reserve it for special highlights.

**Infinite Blue** (Primary/Dominant)
- HEX: `#032D42` · RGB: 3, 45, 66 · PANTONE: 2189 C · CMYK: 100, 74, 47, 44
- **Use:** Dark backgrounds, text on light or white backgrounds

**Wasabi Green** (Accent only)
- HEX: `#63DF4E` · RGB: 99, 223, 78 · PANTONE: 7488 C · CMYK: 56, 0, 92, 0
- **Use:** Key highlights and accents only—never as a background, never for body text

**Black**
- HEX: `#000000` · RGB: 0, 0, 0
- **Use:** Maximum contrast text

**White**
- HEX: `#FFFFFF` · RGB: 255, 255, 255
- **Use:** Text on dark backgrounds

**Neutral Grays** (Supporting)
- `#F9FAFB`—Lightest background (off-white)
- `#F3F4F6`—Light gray backgrounds
- `#E5E7EB`—Borders, dividers
- `#9CA3AF`—Secondary text
- `#6B7280`—Tertiary text
- `#374151`—Dark gray text

### 2.2 Supplementary Palette (Limited Use)

These colors complement our primary palette, adding variety and flexibility without overshadowing Infinite Blue and Wasabi Green. Use sparingly—primarily in gradients, infographics, charts, and data visualizations.

**Bright Blue**
- HEX: `#52B8FF` · RGB: 82, 184, 255 · PANTONE: 298 C · CMYK: 66, 10, 1, 0

**Bright Indigo**
- HEX: `#7661FF` · RGB: 118, 97, 255 · PANTONE: 2725 C · CMYK: 70, 70, 0, 0

**Bright Purple**
- HEX: `#BF71F2` · RGB: 191, 113, 242 · PANTONE: 2572 C · CMYK: 24, 50, 0, 0

### 2.3 Color Don'ts

- ❌ Do not use Wasabi Green as a background color
- ❌ Do not use Wasabi Green against white or light backgrounds (fails accessibility)
- ❌ Do not use supplementary colors as solid backgrounds in large amounts (acceptable in charts and data visualizations)
- ❌ Do not use transparency in solid colors—always use colors at 100% opacity
- ❌ Do not use type/color combinations that fail accessibility
- ❌ Do not use supplementary colors as type—use only primary brand colors for type
- ❌ Do not use Wasabi Green for body copy
- ❌ Do not use colors beyond the primary and supplementary palettes

## 3. Typography

### 3.1 ServiceNow Sans Font Family

ServiceNow Sans is a proprietary typeface with 17 styles across 3 subfamilies. Use Arial as a fallback only when ServiceNow Sans is unavailable, and note the substitution.

**Subfamilies:**
- **ServiceNow Sans (Text)**—Weights: Thin, Light, Book, Regular, Medium, Bold (each with italic). Use for headings, subheadings, and body copy.
- **ServiceNow Sans Display**—Weights: Medium, Bold. Use for headlines and subheadlines only.
- **ServiceNow Sans Mono**—Weight: Regular. Use for eyebrows, category labels, captions, annotations, and code.

**Font styles and when to use them:**

Display Bold → Headlines
Display Medium → Subheadlines
Text Bold → Headings
Text Medium → Subheadings
Text Light → Body copy
Mono Regular → Eyebrows, captions, annotations, data labels

### 3.2 Font Availability & Fallbacks

- **ServiceNow Sans** is the required brand typeface for all outputs
- **Web fonts (.woff2)** are publicly hosted on servicenow.com—downstream skills for HTML/Artifact outputs should include the appropriate `@font-face` declarations
- **Desktop fonts (.ttf)** are bundled in this skill's `assets/fonts/` directory—use these for PNG, PDF, and other non-browser output formats (see Section 3.10 for the full file list)
- **Backup font:** Arial is the approved fallback only when ServiceNow Sans is genuinely unavailable (e.g., PPTX/DOCX where fonts cannot be embedded). Always note the substitution.
- Downstream skills are responsible for format-specific font loading implementation

### 3.3 Type Hierarchy

The size ratio between body copy, subheadline, and headline should be **1:2:4** in most applications. All levels use optical kerning.

| Level | Role | Subfamily | Weight | Tracking | Leading |
|-------|------|-----------|--------|----------|---------|
| 1. Eyebrow | Category labels, overlines | Mono | Regular | 0% | 130% |
| 2. Headline | Primary headline | Display | Bold | 0% | 95% |
| 3. Subheadline | Secondary headline | Display | Medium | 0% | 90% |
| 4. Heading | Section heading | Text | Bold | -1% | 100% |
| 5. Subheading | Subsection heading | Text | Medium | -1% | 100% |
| 6. Body Copy | Paragraph text | Text | Light | 0% | 135% |

### 3.4 Capitalization Rules

- **Sentence case**—Default for most copy, including headlines and body text
- **Title case**—Only for titles of published materials (articles, white papers)
- **ALL CAPS**—Only for Mono-styled copy (eyebrows, category labels)

### 3.5 Two-Tone Headline Treatment (Signature Style)

ServiceNow's hallmark visual pattern. Headlines lead with Wasabi Green, followed by White text.

**Rules:**
1. First portion of headline: **Wasabi Green** (`#63DF4E`)
2. Completing portion: **White** (`#FFFFFF`)
3. Background: **Infinite Blue** (`#032D42`) or a brand gradient
4. Apply to headlines and headings only—never to body copy or mono type
5. **Minimum length:** Do not apply two-tone treatment to headlines shorter than three words—use all White instead

**Example:** "Put AI to work" (Wasabi Green) + "for people" (White) on Infinite Blue background.

**Note:** Reversed dual-tone (White first, then Wasabi Green) is not part of the standard guidelines and requires brand team approval.

### 3.6 Text Color Rules (Hard Rule—No Exceptions)

Always evaluate the background behind the text and apply the correct color:

**On dark backgrounds (Infinite Blue, black, or dark gradients):**
- Headlines: Two-tone treatment (Wasabi Green + White) or all White
- Subheadings, labels, body copy, hex values, descriptions: **White** (`#FFFFFF`)
- Eyebrows and category labels: Wasabi Green (`#63DF4E`)—**dark backgrounds only**
- **Never use gray text on dark backgrounds**—always use full White

**On light backgrounds (white, off-white, light cards, light sections, or any background that appears light due to transparency/alpha):**
- **All text—including eyebrows, Mono labels, and category labels:** **Infinite Blue** (`#032D42`) or **Black** (`#000000`)
- **Never use Wasabi Green text on light backgrounds**—this includes eyebrows and Mono-styled labels. Wasabi Green fails accessibility at 1.85:1 contrast against white. On light backgrounds, eyebrows and labels use Infinite Blue instead.
- **Never use gray text on light backgrounds**—always use Infinite Blue or Black
- **Never use White text on light backgrounds**

**Watch for transparency traps:** If a container uses a semi-transparent fill (e.g., `rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.05)` or `rgba(99, 223, 78, 0.15)`), evaluate the **rendered appearance**, not the code. If the result looks light to the eye, treat it as a light background and use Infinite Blue or Black text. When in doubt, use a fully opaque dark fill instead of a transparent one.

**The rule is absolute:** If the background is dark, use White (with Wasabi Green permitted only for eyebrows). If the background is light, use Infinite Blue or Black for everything—no exceptions for eyebrows, labels, or any other text element. Gray text is never acceptable on any background.

### 3.7 Punctuation in Headlines

Follow standard punctuation rules with one exception: omit the period at the end of short, clear headlines that don't need it. When breaking a headline into multiple lines, break at natural pause points so it remains easy to scan.

- Period adds clarity: "When DHL works, the world works."
- Period not necessary: "Dropbox works with ServiceNow"

### 3.8 Typography Don'ts

- ❌ Do not use non-approved brand fonts
- ❌ Do not create new type style or weight hierarchies
- ❌ Do not use ALL CAPS except for Mono-styled copy (eyebrows, labels)
- ❌ Do not reverse the two-tone treatment (never White first, then Wasabi Green)
- ❌ Do not highlight multiple words out of sequence with Wasabi Green—only highlight complete lines in stacked, two-tone headline text
- ❌ Do not use Wasabi Green text against white or light backgrounds (fails accessibility)
- ❌ Do not right-align text
- ❌ Do not place headlines on busy backgrounds or over the focal point of an image
- ❌ Do not use Wasabi Green for body copy
- ❌ Do not use color to highlight words in body copy—use bold instead

### 3.9 Rounded Corners (All Visual Outputs)

All visual elements use rounded corners. Square corners are off-brand. These rules apply to every output format—HTML, PNG, PDF, presentations, and any generated image.

**Radius scale:**
- **Minimum:** 8px (0.5rem)—smallest acceptable radius for any element
- **Preferred/Cards:** 16px (1rem)—default for cards, containers, and content panels
- **Large containers:** 24px (1.5rem)—outer wrappers, full-image rounding
- **Pill shape:** 9999px—buttons and CTAs only

**Application rules:**
- Cards and content containers: 16px radius
- Color swatches, badges, and small elements: 8px radius minimum
- Overall image or artifact canvas: 24px radius on outer corners
- Buttons: pill shape (fully rounded ends)
- Never use square corners (0px radius) on any visual element
- When elements are adjacent in a strip (e.g., color gradient bar), round only the outer ends

## 4. Skill Inheritance & Downstream Overrides

This top-level skill provides the **brand foundation**. Downstream template skills inherit these standards by default and handle format-specific execution on top of them.

### 4.1 Downstream Skills

- **Corporate PPTX Skill** → Slide decks and presentations
- **Word Doc Skill** → Documents, letters, reports
- **HTML / Artifact Skill** → Dashboards, tools, visualizations, web interfaces
- **Email Signature Skill** → Email signatures
- **Zoom Background Skill** → Virtual meeting backgrounds

### 4.2 The Inheritance Model

Think of this skill as the base stylesheet. Everything in it applies by default to all branded output. Downstream skills act as format-specific overrides—they can add rules and, in limited cases, intentionally deviate from visual design rules when the format demands it.

**The hierarchy:** When a downstream skill explicitly documents an override, the downstream skill wins for that specific rule. For everything else, this top-level skill is the default.

### 4.3 Hard Rules (Never Override)

The following rules are **non-negotiable across all formats and all downstream skills**. No downstream skill may contradict or weaken these, regardless of format or channel:

- **Voice & Tone (all of Section 1)**—The three voice traits, writing rules, craft principles, dos/don'ts, competitive positioning rules, and signature language apply to every piece of content. No exceptions.
- **Brand positioning**—Who we are, why we exist, what we do, how we do it.
- **"People are the heroes" philosophy**—Technology is always the enabler, never the hero.
- **Never name competitors**—Allude to category problems only.
- **No jargon in external-facing content**—No L1/L2/L3, TTR, SLA, MTTR, internal product codes.
- **Always say "people," never "humans."**
- **Accessibility requirements**—Minimum contrast ratios, color not as sole information carrier. (Once Section 2 is populated, specific contrast rules become hard rules.)
- **Core color identity**—Infinite Blue as primary brand color and Wasabi Green as accent-only are non-negotiable. No downstream skill may use Wasabi Green as a background or use off-palette colors.

### 4.4 Soft Rules (Overridable by Downstream Skills)

The following categories **can be overridden** by downstream skills when the format requires it. Any override must be explicitly documented in the downstream skill with a brief rationale.

- **Background treatments**—This top-level skill does not prescribe gradient backgrounds, but the PPTX skill may use Infinite Blue-to-Bright Blue gradients for hero slides, and the HTML/Artifact skill may use gradient backgrounds per servicenow.com conventions.
- **Supplementary color usage**—This skill defines supplementary colors (Bright Blue, Bright Indigo, Bright Purple) as limited-use. Downstream skills may expand their usage for format-specific needs (e.g., HTML skills may use supplementary colors more liberally as section backgrounds or interactive state colors).
- **Layout patterns and density**—A Word doc has different spatial rules than a dashboard. Downstream skills define their own spacing, grid systems, and component density.
- **Component-specific design**—Cards, buttons, badges, navigation elements, and other UI components are defined by the downstream skill for their format, not by this top-level skill.
- **Dark vs. light theme balance**—This skill does not mandate a default theme. Downstream skills choose the appropriate theme split for their format (e.g., PPTX may be primarily dark-on-Infinite-Blue; Word docs may be primarily black-on-white).
- **Typography scale adjustments**—Base font families and weights are set here (once Section 3 is populated), but downstream skills may adjust sizes, line-heights, and scale ratios for their format.

### 4.5 How Downstream Skills Should Document Overrides

Every downstream skill should include a section near the top structured like this:

```
## Relationship to Brand Standards

This skill inherits from `servicenow-brand-standards-reference`. All hard rules
(voice & tone, brand positioning, accessibility, core color identity)
apply without exception.

### Format-specific overrides:
- [Rule being overridden]: [What this skill does instead]—[Why]
- Example: "Background treatments: Uses gradient backgrounds
  (Infinite Blue → Bright Blue blend) for hero and divider slides
 —required for visual consistency with corporate deck templates."
```

This ensures every deviation is intentional, documented, and traceable—not accidental drift from the brand.
